THE REASONS WE EXIST

Farming well shouldn’t mean earning little.

Across Osun State, smallholder tomato and pepper farmers grow crops that feed cities — yet capture only a fraction of what those crops are worth. Fragmented markets, unreliable buyers, and post-harvest losses quietly erode income long before it reaches the market.

HarvCoop gives farmers collective ownership of the infrastructure and relationships built to serve them. Members pool their harvest, their bargaining power, and their voice into one organisation: shared collection points, shared cold storage, and shared market access that no single smallholder could build alone.

01 / TRAINING PROGRAMME
Farmer to farmer

HarvGrow

Hands-on Farmer Field School training in good agricultural practice, post-harvest handling, pest management, and packaging — delivered in Yoruba and English by qualified agronomists, season after season.

Why it matters +

Most tomato and pepper farmers in Osun State have never received formal training in good agronomic practice, pest control, harvest timing, or quality grading. Knowledge that could cut losses and unlock better prices rarely reaches the farmers who need it most.

Yoruba & EnglishQualified agronomistsHands-on learning

02 / DIGITAL PLATFORM
No farmer left out

HarvTrace

A simple digital record of every member’s production, inputs, and deliveries — built to work over 2G and plain SMS, so no farmer is left out for lack of a smartphone or data plan.

Why it matters +

Without any record of what was planted, how it was grown, or where it went, a member has no way to prove their produce meets a buyer’s standard, and no track record to show a lender. Good work goes unrecognised simply because nothing was ever written down.

2G & SMSProduction recordsProof for buyers & lenders

A direct-to-buyer harvest in Osun State proves the case: farmers with a confirmed destination for their produce lose almost nothing to spoilage. The plan for a farmer-owned cooperative begins.
